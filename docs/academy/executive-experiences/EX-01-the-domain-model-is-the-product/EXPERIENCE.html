The Domain Model Is the Product

Which absent field breaks which promise?

90 minutes Facilitated · pairs No technical background required You will not read any code today
1The opening wager5 min

Three questions

Write down three questions a competent system for your practice should be able to answer instantly. Don't discuss them with anyone. You'll come back to these at the end.

2The absence audit15 min

What can't it say?

The fastest way to understand a system is not to list what it does. It is to find what it cannot express. In pairs, attempt each fact below against the system in front of you.

Do not design a fix. Do not debate whether it matters. Record only what happens when you try.

#Try to record this factCan it?
1A hearing — this matter, before this court, on this date☐ Yes ☐ Partly ☐ No
2A court — which forum, and its jurisdiction☐ Yes ☐ Partly ☐ No
3An opposing party — who is on the other side, and who acts for them☐ Yes ☐ Partly ☐ No
4A limitation date — the deadline to bring the claim, and the rule behind it☐ Yes ☐ Partly ☐ No
5A concluded matter — this file is finished, and here is its outcome☐ Yes ☐ Partly ☐ No

One line only. Judged by what it could not say, what did this system turn out to be?

4Promise tracing20 min

What has to exist for this to be true?

Every promise depends on a structure capable of satisfying it. Where that structure is absent, the promise was never translated into the model — and no amount of interface work rescues it.

For each objective, name the entity and the relationship required. Then give a verdict.

7Convergence10 min

What did you actually watch?

Three failures. A missing task. A wrong number. A missing column. Before you open the panel below — and don't open it early — decide what they have in common.

Open when your facilitator says so
A system is defined by its entities and their relationships, not by its screens. Every screen-level failure you saw today is a domain-model failure wearing a costume.
What you sawWhat it actually was
The missing taskA missing relationship — everything dated belongs to a matter
The wrong numberA missing vocabulary — every classification is a controlled list
The missing columnThe same missing relationship, surfacing somewhere else

None of these was a design failure, and none was caught by testing. The dashboard returned the right answer to the query it ran. The screen had nothing to display. The deadline was recorded exactly as entered.

8The question you take back10 min

Your own system

Return to the three questions you wrote at the start. Choose one, and convert it into the form you've been practising all morning:

Which entity and which relationship does this depend on — and do we have them?

That question is the deliverable. It costs nothing to ask, it takes about four minutes to answer, and it is the single cheapest thing you can do to a roadmap before you approve it.
